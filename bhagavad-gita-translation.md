# The Bhagavad Gītā
### A study translation from the Sanskrit

---

## How to read this translation

This is a translation of all 700 verses of the standard text of the *Bhagavad Gītā* (Mahābhārata, Bhīṣma Parvan 23–40). It tries to make the Sanskrit visible through the English. These are the principles it follows:

1. **It translates the text, not a commentary.** Each school (Śaṅkara's Advaita, Rāmānuja's Viśiṣṭādvaita, Madhva's Dvaita, the Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇavas, modern readers like Tilak and Gandhi) reads certain verses its own way. Here the English stays as close as readable English allows to what the Sanskrit says. Where the Sanskrit really is open to more than one reading, a short note says so.
2. **Each key term has one English equivalent throughout.** For example, *buddhi* is always "understanding" and *manas* is always "mind." When the English reads "understanding," the Sanskrit underneath is *buddhi*. The table below lists these pairs.
3. **Sanskrit appears in brackets** where a word carries weight, such as *[dharma]*, *[yoga]* or *[ātman]*. This lets you follow a term from chapter to chapter.
4. **Some words stay in Sanskrit** because no English word holds their range: *dharma, yoga, brahman, guṇa (sattva, rajas, tamas), saṁsāra, māyā, nirvāṇa*.
5. **Epithets are kept.** Kṛṣṇa calls Arjuna "son of Kuntī" or "scorcher of foes," and Arjuna calls Kṛṣṇa "Keśava" or "Madhusūdana." These forms of address set the tone of the dialogue, so they are kept and explained below.
6. **Verse numbers are kept** (chapter.verse). Where the English says "he" for "the yogin" or "the wise," it reflects Sanskrit grammatical gender. It means any person.

## Key terms

| Sanskrit | Rendered as | What it points to |
|---|---|---|
| *ātman* | self | the conscious core of a person; often "the Self" in other translations |
| *dehin* | the embodied one | the self as it lives in a body |
| *buddhi* | understanding | the faculty that discerns and decides; above the mind |
| *manas* | mind | the faculty that receives the senses and wanders |
| *ahaṅkāra* | I-making | the sense "I am the doer / this is mine" |
| *indriya* | senses | the five organs of perception (sometimes also of action) |
| *karma* | action | any deed, ritual or ordinary, and its binding results |
| *phala* | fruit | the outcome or reward of action |
| *saṅga* | attachment | clinging to an act or its outcome |
| *kāma* | desire | craving; also pleasure as a goal of life |
| *yoga* | yoga | literally "yoking": discipline, method, union, skill |
| *yukta* | yoked | disciplined, integrated, concentrated |
| *samatva* | equanimity | sameness of mind in gain and loss |
| *dharma* | dharma | right order, duty, law, one's proper role |
| *svadharma* | one's own dharma | the duty that belongs to one's own nature and station |
| *adharma* | adharma / lawlessness | the breakdown of right order |
| *jñāna* | knowledge | liberating insight |
| *vijñāna* | discernment | knowledge made specific and realized |
| *bhakti* | devotion | loving participation in God |
| *śraddhā* | faith | trust, orientation of the heart |
| *prakṛti* | nature | matter-energy, the field of the guṇas |
| *puruṣa* | person | the conscious principle; in ch. 15, God as Supreme Person |
| *guṇa* | guṇa | the three strands of nature: *sattva* (clarity), *rajas* (passion/activity), *tamas* (inertia/darkness) |
| *brahman* | brahman | the absolute reality; sometimes the Veda (3.15) or primal matter (14.3) |
| *akṣara* | the imperishable | the undying; also "syllable" (Om) |
| *avyakta* | the unmanifest | what has not yet taken form |
| *saṁnyāsa* | renunciation | giving up actions themselves |
| *tyāga* | relinquishment | giving up the fruit of actions |
| *śreyas* | the better / highest good | what is truly good, as against the merely pleasant |
| *vibhūti* | extended power | God's manifest glories in the world |
| *māyā* | māyā | divine power of appearance; bewildering creative power |
| *saṅkalpa* | intention | the mind's willful projecting of goals |
| *loka-saṅgraha* | holding-together of the world | the welfare and cohesion of society |

### Names and epithets

**For Arjuna:** Pārtha (son of Pṛthā, that is, Kuntī); son of Kuntī (Kaunteya); Bhārata (descendant of Bharata); Dhanañjaya (winner of wealth); Guḍākeśa (thick-haired, or master of sleep); Pāṇḍava (son of Pāṇḍu); scorcher of foes (paraṁtapa); mighty-armed one (mahābāhu); joy / best / hero of the Kurus.

**For Kṛṣṇa:** Keśava (fine-haired); Mādhava (descendant of Madhu); Govinda (cow-finder); Janārdana (stirrer of men); Madhusūdana (slayer of the demon Madhu); Hṛṣīkeśa (lord of the senses); Acyuta (unfallen); Vāsudeva (son of Vasudeva); Vārṣṇeya (of the Vṛṣṇi clan); Hari; the Blessed Lord (*Bhagavān*).

**Speakers:** Dhṛtarāṣṭra, the blind Kuru king; Sañjaya, his charioteer, who has been granted divine sight and narrates the battle; Arjuna; and Kṛṣṇa, called "the Blessed Lord."

## Chapter 1 · *Arjuna-viṣāda-yoga* · The Yoga of Arjuna's Despair

*Dhṛtarāṣṭra said:*

**1.1** On the field of dharma, the field of the Kurus [*dharma-kṣetre kuru-kṣetre*], gathered together and eager to fight, what did my sons and the sons of Pāṇḍu do, Sañjaya?

*Sañjaya said:*

**1.2** Seeing the army of the Pāṇḍavas drawn up for battle, King Duryodhana then approached his teacher [*ācārya*, Droṇa] and spoke these words:

**1.3** "Teacher, look at this great army of the sons of Pāṇḍu, arrayed by the son of Drupada, your own gifted student.

**1.4** Here are heroes, great archers, equal in battle to Bhīma and Arjuna: Yuyudhāna, Virāṭa, and Drupada, a great chariot-warrior;

**1.5** Dhṛṣṭaketu, Cekitāna, and the valiant king of Kāśī; Purujit, Kuntibhoja, and Śaibya, a bull among men;

**1.6** bold Yudhāmanyu and valiant Uttamaujas, the son of Subhadrā, and the sons of Draupadī, all of them great chariot-warriors.

**1.7** But know also, best of the twice-born, the most distinguished on our side, the leaders of my army. I name them for your information:

**1.8** yourself and Bhīṣma, Karṇa and Kṛpa, victorious in battle; Aśvatthāman, Vikarṇa, and the son of Somadatta;

**1.9** and many other heroes, ready to give up their lives for my sake, armed with various weapons, all skilled in war.

**1.10** Our strength, guarded by Bhīṣma, is unlimited [*aparyāptam*]; their strength, guarded by Bhīma, is limited [*paryāptam*].
> *Note:* the two words can also mean "insufficient" and "sufficient." Read that way, Duryodhana is privately admitting that his army is weaker. Both readings are old.

**1.11** So all of you, stationed in your positions along every line of advance, protect Bhīṣma above all."

**1.12** To bring him joy, the aged grandsire of the Kurus, full of power, roared a lion's roar and blew his conch.

**1.13** Then conches and kettledrums, cymbals, drums, and horns suddenly sounded, and the noise was tumultuous.

**1.14** Then, standing in their great chariot yoked to white horses, Mādhava [Kṛṣṇa] and the son of Pāṇḍu [Arjuna] blew their divine conches.

**1.15** Hṛṣīkeśa blew Pāñcajanya; Dhanañjaya blew Devadatta; Vṛkodara [Bhīma, "wolf-belly"], of terrible deeds, blew the great conch Pauṇḍra.

**1.16** King Yudhiṣṭhira, son of Kuntī, blew Anantavijaya; Nakula and Sahadeva blew Sughoṣa and Maṇipuṣpaka.

**1.17** The king of Kāśī, supreme archer, and Śikhaṇḍin, the great chariot-warrior, Dhṛṣṭadyumna and Virāṭa and the unconquered Sātyaki,

**1.18** Drupada and the sons of Draupadī, O lord of the earth, and the strong-armed son of Subhadrā, all blew their conches, each his own.

**1.19** That uproar, echoing through sky and earth, tore the hearts of the sons of Dhṛtarāṣṭra.

**1.20** Then, seeing the sons of Dhṛtarāṣṭra drawn up, as the clash of weapons was about to begin, the son of Pāṇḍu, whose banner bears the monkey [Hanumān], raised his bow

**1.21** and spoke these words to Hṛṣīkeśa, O lord of the earth.
*Arjuna said:* Acyuta, place my chariot between the two armies,

**1.22** so that I may look over these men standing here eager for battle, and see with whom I must fight in this undertaking of war.

**1.23** I want to see those who have gathered here ready to fight, wishing to please the evil-minded son of Dhṛtarāṣṭra in battle.

*Sañjaya said:*

**1.24** Addressed thus by Guḍākeśa, O Bhārata, Hṛṣīkeśa stationed that finest of chariots between the two armies,

**1.25** in front of Bhīṣma, Droṇa, and all the rulers of the earth, and said: "Pārtha, behold these Kurus assembled."

**1.26** There the son of Pṛthā saw, standing in both armies, fathers and grandfathers, teachers, maternal uncles, brothers, sons, grandsons, and friends,

**1.27** fathers-in-law and companions. Seeing all these kinsmen arrayed, the son of Kuntī,

**1.28** filled with deep pity [*kṛpā*], said in despair [*viṣīdan*]:
*Arjuna said:* Kṛṣṇa, seeing my own people here, eager to fight, drawn near,

**1.29** my limbs give way, my mouth dries up, my body trembles, and my hair stands on end.

**1.30** Gāṇḍīva [my bow] slips from my hand, my skin burns, I cannot stand still, and my mind seems to whirl.

**1.31** I see adverse omens, Keśava, and I foresee no good [*śreyas*] in killing my own people in battle.

**1.32** I do not want victory, Kṛṣṇa, nor kingship nor pleasures. What use is kingship to us, Govinda, or enjoyments, or even life?

**1.33** Those for whose sake we desire kingship, enjoyments, and pleasures stand here in battle, having given up life and wealth:

**1.34** teachers, fathers, sons, and grandfathers too, maternal uncles, fathers-in-law, grandsons, brothers-in-law, and other kinsmen.

**1.35** I do not wish to kill them, Madhusūdana, even if they kill me. I would not do it even for sovereignty over the three worlds, much less for the earth.

**1.36** What joy could be ours, Janārdana, after killing the sons of Dhṛtarāṣṭra? Evil [*pāpa*] alone would cling to us for slaying these aggressors [*ātatāyin*].

**1.37** So we ought not to kill the sons of Dhṛtarāṣṭra, our own kin. How could we be happy, Mādhava, having killed our own people?

**1.38** Their minds are overpowered by greed, and they do not see the wrong in destroying a family or the crime in betraying friends.

**1.39** But why should we not have the sense to turn back from this evil, Janārdana, we who do see the wrong in destroying a family?

**1.40** When a family is destroyed, its timeless family dharmas perish. When dharma perishes, adharma overcomes the whole family.

**1.41** When adharma prevails, Kṛṣṇa, the women of the family are corrupted. When the women are corrupted, Vārṣṇeya, the social orders become mixed [*varṇa-saṅkara*].

**1.42** This mixing leads both the family and its destroyers to hell, for their ancestors fall, deprived of the offerings of rice-balls and water.

**1.43** By these wrongs of the family-destroyers, which cause the mixing of social orders, the timeless dharmas of birth-group [*jāti*] and family [*kula*] are destroyed.

**1.44** For men whose family dharmas are destroyed, Janārdana, a dwelling in hell is certain. So we have heard.

**1.45** Alas! We are resolved to commit a great evil, ready to kill our own people out of greed for the pleasures of kingship.

**1.46** If the sons of Dhṛtarāṣṭra, weapons in hand, were to kill me in battle, unresisting and unarmed, that would be better for me.

*Sañjaya said:*

**1.47** Having spoken thus on the battlefield, Arjuna sank down on the seat of the chariot, letting fall his bow and arrows, his mind overwhelmed by grief.

## Chapter 2 · *Sāṅkhya-yoga* · The Yoga of Understanding

*Sañjaya said:*

**2.1** To him, thus overcome with pity, his eyes filled with tears and troubled, sinking in despair, Madhusūdana spoke these words.

*The Blessed Lord said:*

**2.2** From where has this faintness [*kaśmala*] come upon you at this moment of crisis, Arjuna? It is not the way of the noble [*anārya-juṣṭa*, "followed by the ignoble"]. It does not lead to heaven and it brings disgrace.

**2.3** Do not yield to unmanliness [*klaibya*], Pārtha; it does not suit you. Cast off this petty weakness of heart and stand up, scorcher of foes!

*Arjuna said:*

**2.4** How can I fight Bhīṣma and Droṇa in battle with arrows, Madhusūdana? They are worthy of worship, slayer of enemies.

**2.5** Better to live in this world on alms than to kill these honored teachers. If I kill my teachers, even though they want gain, I would enjoy here only pleasures smeared with blood.
> *Note:* *artha-kāmān* can describe the teachers ("who seek gain") or the pleasures ("pleasures of wealth and desire").

**2.6** Nor do we know which is better for us: that we conquer them or that they conquer us. The sons of Dhṛtarāṣṭra stand before us, and if we killed them we would not wish to live.

**2.7** My very being is struck by the flaw of pity [*kārpaṇya-doṣa*], and my mind is confused about dharma. I ask you: tell me decisively what is better [*śreyas*]. I am your student. Instruct me, for I have taken refuge in you.

**2.8** I do not see what would drive away this grief that dries up my senses, even if I won unrivaled, prosperous kingship on earth, or even lordship over the gods.

*Sañjaya said:*

**2.9** Having spoken thus to Hṛṣīkeśa, Guḍākeśa, scorcher of foes, said to Govinda, "I will not fight," and fell silent.

**2.10** Then, O Bhārata, between the two armies, Hṛṣīkeśa, as if smiling, spoke these words to him as he sank in despair.

*The Blessed Lord said:*

**2.11** You grieve for those who should not be grieved for, and yet you speak words of wisdom. The wise [*paṇḍita*] grieve neither for the dead nor for the living.

**2.12** Never was there a time when I did not exist, nor you, nor these rulers of men. Nor will there ever be a time hereafter when we all shall cease to be.

**2.13** In this body the embodied one [*dehin*] passes through childhood, youth, and old age. In the same way it passes to another body. The steadfast [*dhīra*] are not bewildered by this.

**2.14** Contacts of the senses with their objects [*mātrā-sparśa*], son of Kuntī, give rise to cold and heat, pleasure and pain. They come and go and are impermanent. Endure them, Bhārata.

**2.15** The person whom these do not disturb, bull among men, who is the same in pain and pleasure and steadfast, is fit for immortality [*amṛtatva*].

**2.16** Of the unreal [*asat*] there is no coming to be; of the real [*sat*] there is no ceasing to be. Those who see the truth [*tattva-darśin*] have seen where the two meet.

**2.17** Know that to be indestructible by which all this is pervaded. No one can destroy this imperishable.

**2.18** The embodied one is eternal, indestructible, and immeasurable, but its bodies are said to come to an end. Therefore fight, Bhārata.

**2.19** One who thinks this a killer and one who thinks it killed both fail to understand. It does not kill, and it is not killed.

**2.20** It is never born, nor does it ever die. It did not come into being, nor will it cease to be. Unborn, eternal, everlasting, primeval, it is not killed when the body is killed.
> *Note:* 2.19–20 closely echo *Kaṭha Upaniṣad* 2.18–19.

**2.21** One who knows it to be indestructible, eternal, unborn, and unchanging: how can that person kill anyone, Pārtha, or cause anyone to kill?

**2.22** As a person casts off worn-out clothes and puts on new ones, so the embodied one casts off worn-out bodies and enters new ones.

**2.23** Weapons do not cut it, fire does not burn it, water does not wet it, wind does not dry it.

**2.24** It cannot be cut, burned, wetted, or dried. It is eternal, all-pervading, stable, unmoving, everlasting.

**2.25** It is called unmanifest [*avyakta*], unthinkable, unchangeable. Therefore, knowing it to be such, you should not grieve.

**2.26** And even if you think it is constantly born and constantly dies, even then, mighty-armed one, you should not grieve.

**2.27** For death is certain for one who is born, and birth is certain for one who has died. Therefore you should not grieve over what cannot be avoided.

**2.28** Beings are unmanifest in their beginning, manifest in their middle, Bhārata, and unmanifest again in their end. What is there to lament?

**2.29** Someone sees it as a marvel; another speaks of it as a marvel; another hears of it as a marvel. Yet even having heard of it, no one knows it.

**2.30** This embodied one in the body of everyone can never be harmed, Bhārata. Therefore you should not grieve for any being.

**2.31** Look also to your own dharma [*svadharma*] and do not waver. For a warrior [*kṣatriya*] nothing is better than a battle fought in accord with dharma.

**2.32** Happy are the warriors, Pārtha, who meet such a battle by chance, an open door to heaven.

**2.33** But if you will not fight this battle of dharma, you will abandon your own dharma and your honor and incur evil.

**2.34** People will speak of your undying disgrace, and for one who has been honored, disgrace is worse than death.

**2.35** The great chariot-warriors will think you withdrew from battle out of fear. They held you in high esteem, and now they will think little of you.

**2.36** Your enemies will say many unspeakable things, mocking your ability. What could be more painful than that?

**2.37** Either you will be killed and attain heaven, or you will conquer and enjoy the earth. So stand up, son of Kuntī, resolved to fight.

**2.38** Treat alike pleasure and pain, gain and loss, victory and defeat, and gird yourself for battle. In this way you will not incur evil.

**2.39** This understanding [*buddhi*] has been declared to you according to Sāṅkhya. Now hear it according to yoga. Joined to this understanding, Pārtha, you will throw off the bondage of action [*karma-bandha*].

**2.40** In this no effort is lost and no setback occurs. Even a little of this dharma protects from great fear.

**2.41** Here, joy of the Kurus, the understanding whose essence is resolve [*vyavasāyātmikā buddhi*] is single. The understandings of the irresolute are many-branched and endless.

**2.42** The undiscerning delight in the letter of the Veda [*veda-vāda*], Pārtha, and say "there is nothing else." They utter flowery speech,

**2.43** full of desire and intent on heaven. That speech offers rebirth as the fruit of action and abounds in special rites aimed at enjoyment and power.

**2.44** Those attached to enjoyment and power have their minds carried off by that speech. In them the understanding whose essence is resolve is not established in concentration [*samādhi*].

**2.45** The Vedas have the three guṇas as their domain. Be free of the three guṇas, Arjuna: free from the pairs of opposites [*dvandva*], always established in clarity [*nitya-sattva-stha*], free of acquiring and keeping, possessed of the self.

**2.46** When there is a flood of water on every side, a well is of little use. All the Vedas are of as little use to a brāhmaṇa who truly understands.

**2.47** Your right [*adhikāra*] is to action alone, never to its fruits. Do not let the fruit of action be your motive, and do not be attached to inaction.

**2.48** Steadfast in yoga, perform actions, Dhanañjaya. Abandon attachment and be the same in success and failure. Equanimity [*samatva*] is called yoga.

**2.49** Action by itself is far inferior to the yoga of understanding [*buddhi-yoga*], Dhanañjaya. Seek refuge in understanding. Pitiable are those whose motive is the fruit.

**2.50** One joined to understanding leaves behind, here and now, both good and bad deeds. Therefore yoke yourself to yoga. Yoga is skill in actions [*yogaḥ karmasu kauśalam*].

**2.51** The wise, joined to understanding, give up the fruit born of action. Freed from the bondage of birth, they go to the place beyond sorrow.

**2.52** When your understanding crosses beyond the thicket of delusion, you will become indifferent to what you will hear and what you have heard.

**2.53** Your understanding is now bewildered by what you have heard [*śruti*, the Veda]. When it stands unmoving, stable in concentration, you will attain yoga.

*Arjuna said:*

**2.54** What is the description of one whose wisdom is steady [*sthita-prajña*], who is established in concentration, Keśava? How does one of steady thought speak? How does he sit? How does he move?

*The Blessed Lord said:*

**2.55** One who abandons all desires that have entered the mind, Pārtha, and is content in the self by the self alone, is called steady in wisdom.

**2.56** One whose mind is not shaken in sorrows and who has no craving in pleasures, from whom passion, fear, and anger have departed: that sage [*muni*] is called steady in thought.

**2.57** One who is unattached everywhere, who neither rejoices nor hates on meeting this or that, good or bad: that one's wisdom is established.

**2.58** When one withdraws the senses from their objects on every side, as a tortoise draws in its limbs, that one's wisdom is established.

**2.59** Sense objects fall away from the embodied one who does not feed on them, but the taste for them [*rasa*] remains. Even the taste falls away once the supreme has been seen.

**2.60** Even for the wise person who strives, son of Kuntī, the turbulent senses forcibly carry off the mind.

**2.61** Restraining them all, one should sit yoked, intent on me. For one whose senses are under control, wisdom is established.

**2.62** When a person dwells on the objects of the senses, attachment to them arises. From attachment arises desire [*kāma*]; from desire arises anger;

**2.63** from anger comes delusion; from delusion, confusion of memory; from confusion of memory, loss of understanding; and from loss of understanding, one perishes.

**2.64** But one who moves among the objects of the senses with senses free from attraction and aversion and governed by the self, self-mastered, attains serenity [*prasāda*].

**2.65** In serenity all one's sorrows come to an end, for when the mind is serene, the understanding quickly becomes steady.

**2.66** The unyoked have no understanding and no power of meditation [*bhāvanā*]. Without meditation there is no peace, and without peace, where is happiness?

**2.67** When the mind follows the roaming senses, it carries away one's wisdom, as the wind carries a ship on the water.

**2.68** Therefore, mighty-armed one, one whose senses are entirely withdrawn from their objects has established wisdom.

**2.69** What is night for all beings, the self-controlled one is awake to. What beings are awake to is night for the sage who sees.

**2.70** The waters flow into the ocean, which is ever being filled yet stays unmoved in its depth. The one into whom all desires enter in that way attains peace, not the one who desires desires.

**2.71** The person who gives up all desires and moves about free of longing, without "mine" and without I-making [*ahaṅkāra*], attains peace.

**2.72** This is the state of brahman [*brāhmī sthiti*], Pārtha. Having attained it, one is not deluded. Remaining in it even at the hour of death, one reaches the nirvāṇa of brahman [*brahma-nirvāṇa*].

## Chapter 3 · *Karma-yoga* · The Yoga of Action

*Arjuna said:*

**3.1** If you hold that understanding is superior to action, Janārdana, then why do you urge me to this terrible action, Keśava?

**3.2** Your words seem to conflict, and they confuse my understanding. Tell me decisively the one thing by which I may reach the better [*śreyas*].

*The Blessed Lord said:*

**3.3** In this world, sinless one, I taught long ago a twofold foundation: the yoga of knowledge [*jñāna-yoga*] for followers of Sāṅkhya, and the yoga of action [*karma-yoga*] for yogins.

**3.4** A person does not reach freedom from action [*naiṣkarmya*] by not undertaking actions, nor reach perfection by renunciation [*saṁnyāsa*] alone.

**3.5** No one can remain even for a moment without acting. The guṇas born of nature [*prakṛti*] make everyone act, whether they will it or not.

**3.6** One who restrains the organs of action but sits remembering sense objects with the mind is a deluded self, called a hypocrite.

**3.7** But one who controls the senses with the mind, Arjuna, and without attachment undertakes the yoga of action with the organs of action, excels.

**3.8** Perform your prescribed [*niyata*] action, for action is better than inaction. Without action you could not even maintain your body.

**3.9** This world is bound by action, except for action done for the sake of sacrifice [*yajña*]. Perform action for that purpose, son of Kuntī, free from attachment.

**3.10** Long ago, having created creatures together with sacrifice, the Lord of creatures [*Prajāpati*] said: "By this you shall multiply. Let this be your wish-fulfilling cow.

**3.11** Nourish the gods with this, and let the gods nourish you. Nourishing one another, you will reach the highest good.

**3.12** Nourished by sacrifice, the gods will give you the enjoyments you desire." One who enjoys their gifts without giving to them in return is simply a thief.

**3.13** The good, who eat what remains from the sacrifice, are released from all faults. The wicked, who cook only for themselves, eat evil.

**3.14** Beings arise from food; food arises from rain; rain arises from sacrifice; sacrifice arises from action.

**3.15** Know that action arises from brahman [here, the Veda], and brahman arises from the imperishable. Therefore the all-pervading brahman is always established in sacrifice.

**3.16** One who does not keep turning the wheel set in motion here lives an evil life, delighting in the senses. That one lives in vain, Pārtha.

**3.17** But the person who delights in the self alone, content in the self and satisfied in the self alone, has nothing that must be done.

**3.18** That person gains nothing by acting here and nothing by not acting, and depends on no being for any purpose.

**3.19** Therefore, without attachment, always perform the action that must be done. By performing action without attachment, a person reaches the highest.

**3.20** Janaka and others attained perfection through action alone. You too should act, looking to the holding-together of the world [*loka-saṅgraha*].

**3.21** Whatever the best person does, others do too. Whatever standard that one sets, the world follows.

**3.22** For me, Pārtha, there is nothing whatever to be done in the three worlds, and nothing unattained to attain. Yet I continue in action.

**3.23** For if I ever ceased to act tirelessly, Pārtha, people would follow my path in every way.

**3.24** If I did not perform action, these worlds would perish. I would cause confusion and destroy these creatures.

**3.25** The unwise act attached to action, Bhārata. The wise should act in the same way but without attachment, wishing to hold the world together.

**3.26** The wise should not unsettle the understanding of the ignorant who are attached to action. Acting with yoga themselves, they should lead them to delight in all actions.

**3.27** Actions are performed in every case by the guṇas of nature, but one deluded by I-making thinks, "I am the doer."

**3.28** But one who knows the truth about how the guṇas and their actions are divided, mighty-armed one, thinks "guṇas are moving among guṇas" and is not attached.

**3.29** Those deluded by the guṇas of nature are attached to the actions of the guṇas. One who knows the whole should not unsettle the slow-witted who know only in part.

**3.30** Surrender all actions to me with your mind on the inner self [*adhyātma*]. Free from longing and from "mine," fight, with your fever gone.

**3.31** Those who always follow this teaching of mine, full of faith and without grumbling, are also freed from actions.

**3.32** But those who grumble at it and do not follow my teaching are bewildered in all knowledge. Know them to be lost and mindless.

**3.33** Even the wise person acts according to his own nature. Beings follow nature. What will restraint accomplish?

**3.34** Attraction and aversion lie in wait in the objects of each sense. One should not come under their power, for they are one's enemies on the road.

**3.35** Better one's own dharma, though imperfect, than another's dharma well performed. Better death in one's own dharma; another's dharma is dangerous.

*Arjuna said:*

**3.36** Then what drives a person to commit evil, Vārṣṇeya, even unwillingly, as if forced?

*The Blessed Lord said:*

**3.37** It is desire, it is anger, born of the guṇa of rajas: all-devouring and greatly evil. Know it as the enemy here.

**3.38** As fire is covered by smoke, a mirror by dust, and an embryo by the womb, so this [knowledge] is covered by that [desire].

**3.39** Knowledge is covered, son of Kuntī, by this constant enemy of the wise, which takes the form of desire, an insatiable fire.

**3.40** The senses, the mind, and the understanding are said to be its seat. Through these it covers knowledge and deludes the embodied one.

**3.41** Therefore, bull of the Bhāratas, first control the senses. Then kill this evil thing, which destroys knowledge and discernment [*jñāna-vijñāna*].

**3.42** The senses are high, they say. Higher than the senses is the mind; higher than the mind is the understanding; but what is higher than the understanding is *he* [the self].

**3.43** Knowing what is higher than the understanding, steady the self by the self, mighty-armed one, and kill the enemy that takes the form of desire, so hard to overcome.

---

## Chapter 4 · *Jñāna-karma-saṁnyāsa-yoga* · The Yoga of Knowledge and the Renunciation of Action

*The Blessed Lord said:*

**4.1** I taught this imperishable yoga to Vivasvat [the sun]. Vivasvat told it to Manu, and Manu told it to Ikṣvāku.

**4.2** Handed down in this succession, the royal seers knew it. But with the long passage of time, scorcher of foes, this yoga was lost here.

**4.3** Today I have declared this same ancient yoga to you, for you are my devotee and my friend. This is the supreme secret.

*Arjuna said:*

**4.4** Your birth was later and Vivasvat's was earlier. How am I to understand that you taught it in the beginning?

*The Blessed Lord said:*

**4.5** Many births of mine have passed, Arjuna, and many of yours. I know them all, but you do not know them, scorcher of foes.

**4.6** Though I am unborn and my self is imperishable, though I am lord of beings, I come into being by my own power [*ātma-māyā*], governing my own nature.

**4.7** Whenever dharma declines, Bhārata, and adharma rises, then I send myself forth.

**4.8** To protect the good, to destroy evildoers, and to establish dharma, I come into being age after age [*yuge yuge*].

**4.9** One who truly knows my divine birth and action is not born again on leaving the body. That one comes to me, Arjuna.

**4.10** Free from passion, fear, and anger, absorbed in me and taking refuge in me, purified by the austerity of knowledge, many have come to my state of being.

**4.11** In whatever way people approach me, in that way I share myself with them [*bhajāmi*]. Everywhere, Pārtha, people follow my path.

**4.12** Wanting success from their actions, people here sacrifice to the gods, for in the human world success born of action comes quickly.

**4.13** I created the four social orders [*cātur-varṇya*] according to the division of guṇa and action. Though I am their maker, know me as the non-doer, imperishable.
> *Note:* The verse grounds the four social orders in *guṇa* and *karma* (quality and action), not explicitly in birth. Readers have long debated whether birth is implied.

**4.14** Actions do not taint me, and I have no longing for their fruit. One who knows me in this way is not bound by actions.

**4.15** Knowing this, the ancient seekers of liberation also performed action. So perform action, as the ancients did long ago.

**4.16** What is action? What is inaction? Even the wise are confused here. I will explain action to you, and knowing it you will be freed from evil.

**4.17** One must understand action, understand wrong action [*vikarma*], and understand inaction. The way of action is deep.

**4.18** One who sees inaction in action and action in inaction is wise among humans. That one is yoked, even while performing all action.

**4.19** When all of a person's undertakings are free of desire and intention [*saṅkalpa*], and that person's actions are burned up in the fire of knowledge, the wise call that person learned.

**4.20** Abandoning attachment to the fruit of action, always content and dependent on nothing, such a person does nothing at all, even while engaged in action.

**4.21** Without longing, with mind and self restrained, giving up all possessions, doing action with the body alone, one incurs no fault.

**4.22** Content with whatever comes by chance, beyond the pairs of opposites, free from envy, the same in success and failure, such a person is not bound even when acting.

**4.23** When a person's attachment is gone and he is free, with mind established in knowledge and acting for the sake of sacrifice, his action dissolves entirely.

**4.24** The offering is brahman; the oblation is brahman, poured by brahman into the fire that is brahman. One who sees brahman in action, as in concentration, will reach brahman.

**4.25** Some yogins offer sacrifice to the gods alone; others offer sacrifice, by sacrifice itself, into the fire of brahman.

**4.26** Some offer hearing and the other senses into the fires of restraint; others offer sound and the other objects into the fires of the senses.

**4.27** Others offer all the actions of the senses and of the vital breath into the fire of the yoga of self-restraint, kindled by knowledge.

**4.28** Others sacrifice with material goods, with austerity, or with yoga, and ascetics of strict vows sacrifice through study and knowledge.

**4.29** Others, intent on breath-control, offer the in-breath into the out-breath and the out-breath into the in-breath, restraining the course of both.

**4.30** Others, restricting their food, offer breaths into breaths. All of these know sacrifice, and their faults are destroyed by sacrifice.

**4.31** Eating the nectar that remains from sacrifice, they go to eternal brahman. This world is not for one who does not sacrifice, best of Kurus, much less the next.

**4.32** Many kinds of sacrifice are spread out like this in the mouth of brahman. Know that they are all born of action. Knowing this, you will be freed.

**4.33** Sacrifice made of knowledge is better than sacrifice of material things, scorcher of foes. All action without exception, Pārtha, culminates in knowledge.

**4.34** Learn this by bowing down, by inquiry, and by service. The wise who see the truth will teach you knowledge.

**4.35** Knowing it, Pāṇḍava, you will not fall into delusion like this again. By it you will see all beings without exception in the self, and then in me.

**4.36** Even if you were the worst of all evildoers, the boat of knowledge alone would carry you across all wickedness.

**4.37** As a kindled fire reduces its fuel to ashes, Arjuna, so the fire of knowledge reduces all actions to ashes.

**4.38** Nothing here purifies like knowledge. One perfected in yoga finds it, in time, within oneself.

**4.39** One who has faith [*śraddhā*], is intent on knowledge, and restrains the senses gains it. Having gained knowledge, one soon reaches supreme peace.

**4.40** The ignorant self, without faith and full of doubt, perishes. For the doubter there is neither this world, nor the next, nor happiness.

**4.41** Actions do not bind one who has renounced action through yoga, whose doubt has been cut away by knowledge, and who possesses the self, Dhanañjaya.

**4.42** So take the sword of knowledge and cut this doubt in your heart, born of ignorance. Resort to yoga. Stand up, Bhārata!

## Chapter 5 · *Karma-saṁnyāsa-yoga* · The Yoga of Renunciation

*Arjuna said:*

**5.1** You praise renunciation of actions, Kṛṣṇa, and then again yoga. Tell me decisively which of the two is better.

*The Blessed Lord said:*

**5.2** Renunciation and the yoga of action both lead to the highest good. But of the two, the yoga of action is better than renunciation of action.

**5.3** Know as a constant renunciant the one who neither hates nor desires. Free of the pairs of opposites, mighty-armed one, one is easily freed from bondage.

**5.4** Children, not the wise, speak of Sāṅkhya and yoga as different. One truly established in either finds the fruit of both.

**5.5** The yogins reach the same state that the followers of Sāṅkhya reach. One who sees Sāṅkhya and yoga as one truly sees.

**5.6** But renunciation, mighty-armed one, is hard to attain without yoga. The sage who is yoked in yoga soon reaches brahman.

**5.7** One yoked in yoga is pure in self, self-conquered, and master of the senses. When one's self has become the self of all beings, one is not tainted even when acting.

**5.8** The yoked knower of truth thinks, "I do nothing at all." While seeing, hearing, touching, smelling, eating, walking, sleeping, breathing,

**5.9** speaking, releasing, grasping, opening and closing the eyes, such a one holds that only the senses are moving among the objects of the senses.

**5.10** One who acts while placing all actions in brahman and abandoning attachment is not stained by evil, just as a lotus leaf is not stained by water.

**5.11** To purify themselves, yogins abandon attachment and perform action with the body, the mind, the understanding, or even the senses alone.

**5.12** The yoked one, abandoning the fruit of action, attains abiding peace. The unyoked one, attached to the fruit and driven by desire, is bound.

**5.13** Renouncing all actions with the mind, the embodied one sits happily in control, in the city of nine gates [the body], neither acting nor causing action.

**5.14** The Lord [*prabhu*] does not create agency or actions for the world, nor the link between action and fruit. It is inherent nature [*svabhāva*] that is at work.

**5.15** The all-pervading one does not take on anyone's evil or good deed. Knowledge is covered by ignorance, and that is why beings are deluded.

**5.16** But for those in whom ignorance is destroyed by knowledge of the self, that knowledge lights up the supreme, like the sun.

**5.17** Their understanding is on that, their self in that, they are established in that and intent on that. Their faults cleansed by knowledge, they go where there is no return.

**5.18** The wise see the same in a learned and humble brāhmaṇa, in a cow, an elephant, a dog, and an outcaste [*śva-pāka*, "one who cooks dogs"].

**5.19** Even here, those whose minds rest in sameness have overcome the cycle of creation. Brahman is flawless and the same in all, so they are established in brahman.

**5.20** One should not rejoice on getting what is pleasant nor be shaken on getting what is unpleasant. Steady in understanding and undeluded, the knower of brahman is established in brahman.

**5.21** With the self unattached to outer contacts, one finds happiness in the self. With the self yoked in the yoga of brahman, one reaches imperishable happiness.

**5.22** The enjoyments born of contact are wombs of sorrow. They have a beginning and an end, son of Kuntī, and the wise do not delight in them.

**5.23** One who can withstand, here before leaving the body, the agitation born of desire and anger, is yoked. That one is a happy person.

**5.24** The yogin whose happiness is within, whose delight is within, and whose light is within becomes brahman and reaches the nirvāṇa of brahman.

**5.25** Seers whose faults are destroyed and whose doubts are cut away, who are self-controlled and delight in the welfare of all beings, reach the nirvāṇa of brahman.

**5.26** For ascetics who are free of desire and anger, whose thought is controlled and who know the self, the nirvāṇa of brahman is near on every side.

**5.27** Shutting out outer contacts, fixing the gaze between the eyebrows, and evening out the in-breath and out-breath that move within the nostrils,

**5.28** the sage whose senses, mind, and understanding are controlled, who is intent on release, and from whom desire, fear, and anger have departed, is forever free.

**5.29** Knowing me as the enjoyer of sacrifices and austerities, the great lord of all the worlds, the friend of all beings, one reaches peace.

## Chapter 6 · *Dhyāna-yoga* · The Yoga of Meditation

*The Blessed Lord said:*

**6.1** One who performs the action that should be done, without depending on its fruit, is a renunciant and a yogin. One who merely gives up the sacred fires and the rites is not.

**6.2** What they call renunciation, Pāṇḍava, know to be yoga. No one becomes a yogin without renouncing intention [*saṅkalpa*].

**6.3** For the sage who wishes to climb to yoga, action is said to be the means. Once that same sage has climbed to yoga, calm [*śama*] is said to be the means.

**6.4** When one is not attached to sense objects or to actions and has renounced all intentions, one is said to have climbed to yoga.

**6.5** One should lift up the self by the self and not let the self sink down. For the self alone is the self's friend, and the self alone is the self's enemy.

**6.6** For one who has conquered the self by the self, the self is a friend. But for one who has not conquered the self, the self acts with hostility, like an enemy.

**6.7** For one who is self-conquered and at peace, the supreme self stays concentrated in cold and heat, pleasure and pain, honor and dishonor.

**6.8** A yogin is called yoked when the self is satisfied with knowledge and discernment, unshakable, and master of the senses, and when a clod, a stone, and gold are all the same to that person.

**6.9** One who regards with an equal mind well-wishers, friends, enemies, the indifferent, the neutral, the hateful, and relatives, and the good and the wicked alike, excels.

**6.10** The yogin should constantly concentrate the self, staying in solitude and alone, with thought and self controlled, free of longing and without possessions.

**6.11** In a clean place, he should set up for himself a firm seat, neither too high nor too low, covered with cloth, deerskin, and kuśa grass.

**6.12** Sitting there, making the mind one-pointed and controlling the activity of thought and senses, he should practice yoga to purify the self.

**6.13** He should hold body, head, and neck straight and still, steady, gazing at the tip of his nose and not looking around.

**6.14** With the self at peace, free of fear, firm in the vow of celibacy [*brahmacarya*], controlling the mind, he should sit yoked, with his thought on me and intent on me.

**6.15** Always concentrating the self in this way, the yogin with controlled mind reaches the peace that culminates in nirvāṇa and abides in me.

**6.16** But yoga is not for one who eats too much or for one who does not eat at all, Arjuna, nor for one who sleeps too much or stays awake too long.

**6.17** For one who is moderate in eating and recreation, moderate in effort in action, and moderate in sleep and waking, yoga destroys sorrow.

**6.18** When thought is well controlled and rests in the self alone, free from longing for any desire, one is said to be yoked.

**6.19** "Like a lamp in a windless place, which does not flicker": this is the image given for the yogin whose thought is controlled, practicing the yoga of the self.

**6.20** Thought comes to rest, restrained by the practice of yoga, and seeing the self by the self, one is content in the self.

**6.21** One knows that infinite happiness which the understanding grasps and which lies beyond the senses. Established there, one does not move from the truth.

**6.22** Having gained it, one thinks no other gain greater. Established in it, one is not shaken even by heavy sorrow.

**6.23** Let that be known as yoga: the unyoking from union with sorrow [*duḥkha-saṁyoga-viyoga*]. That yoga should be practiced with resolve and with a mind that does not lose heart.

**6.24** Abandon entirely all desires born of intention, and with the mind alone hold back the whole group of senses on every side.

**6.25** Little by little one should come to rest, with the understanding held firm. Having made the mind abide in the self, one should think of nothing at all.

**6.26** Whenever the restless, unsteady mind wanders off, one should rein it back from there and bring it under the control of the self.

**6.27** Supreme happiness comes to the yogin whose mind is tranquil, whose passion is stilled, who has become brahman and is free of fault.

**6.28** Constantly concentrating the self in this way, the yogin, free of fault, easily attains the infinite happiness of contact with brahman.

**6.29** One whose self is yoked in yoga and who sees the same everywhere sees the self in all beings and all beings in the self.

**6.30** One who sees me everywhere and sees everything in me: I am not lost to that one, and that one is not lost to me.

**6.31** The yogin who is established in oneness and worships me as dwelling in all beings abides in me, however he lives.

**6.32** One who sees the same in everything by comparison with oneself [*ātmaupamya*], Arjuna, whether pleasure or pain, is considered the supreme yogin.
> *Note:* *ātmaupamya*, "taking oneself as the measure," is the Gītā's version of the golden rule. Others' pleasure and pain are felt as one's own.

*Arjuna said:*

**6.33** You have taught this yoga as sameness, Madhusūdana, but because of restlessness I do not see how it can stand firm.

**6.34** For the mind is restless, Kṛṣṇa, turbulent, strong, and stubborn. I think it is as hard to restrain as the wind.

*The Blessed Lord said:*

**6.35** No doubt, mighty-armed one, the mind is restless and hard to restrain. But it is held by practice [*abhyāsa*] and dispassion [*vairāgya*], son of Kuntī.

**6.36** I agree that yoga is hard to attain for one whose self is uncontrolled. But one whose self is controlled and who strives can attain it by the right means.

*Arjuna said:*

**6.37** Consider one who has faith but is not controlled, whose mind strays from yoga before reaching perfection in yoga. What path does that one take, Kṛṣṇa?

**6.38** Fallen from both, without support, bewildered on the path to brahman, does he not perish like a scattered cloud, mighty-armed one?

**6.39** Cut away this doubt of mine completely, Kṛṣṇa, for no one but you can cut it away.

*The Blessed Lord said:*

**6.40** Pārtha, there is no destruction for him, either here or hereafter. No one who does good, my friend, comes to a bad end.

**6.41** The one fallen from yoga reaches the worlds of the meritorious and dwells there for countless years. Then he is born in the house of the pure and prosperous.

**6.42** Or he is born into a family of wise yogins, though such a birth is harder to obtain in this world.

**6.43** There he regains the understanding he reached in his former body, joy of the Kurus, and from there strives again for perfection.

**6.44** His former practice carries him along even without his willing it. Even one who only wishes to know yoga goes beyond the word-brahman [the Veda].

**6.45** But the yogin who strives with effort is purified of faults and perfected through many births. Then he reaches the supreme goal.

**6.46** The yogin is superior to ascetics, superior even to the learned, and superior to those who perform ritual action. Therefore be a yogin, Arjuna.

**6.47** And of all yogins, I consider most fully yoked the one who is full of faith and worships me with the inner self absorbed in me.

## Chapter 7 · *Jñāna-vijñāna-yoga* · The Yoga of Knowledge and Discernment

*The Blessed Lord said:*

**7.1** Hear, Pārtha, how you will know me completely and without doubt, with your mind attached to me, practicing yoga and relying on me.

**7.2** I will teach you this knowledge together with discernment [*jñāna-vijñāna*] in full. Once you know it, nothing further remains to be known here.

**7.3** Among thousands of people, scarcely one strives for perfection. Of those who strive and are perfected, scarcely one knows me in truth.

**7.4** Earth, water, fire, wind, space, mind, understanding, and I-making: this is my nature [*prakṛti*], divided eightfold.

**7.5** This is my lower nature. But know my other, higher nature, mighty-armed one: the principle of life [*jīva-bhūta*], by which this world is sustained.

**7.6** Understand that all beings have these two as their source. I am the origin and the dissolution of the whole world.

**7.7** There is nothing whatever higher than me, Dhanañjaya. All this is strung on me like pearls on a thread.

**7.8** I am the taste in water, son of Kuntī, and the light in moon and sun. I am the syllable Om in all the Vedas, the sound in space, the manhood in men.

**7.9** I am the pure fragrance in the earth and the brilliance in fire. I am the life in all beings and the austerity in ascetics.

**7.10** Know me, Pārtha, as the eternal seed of all beings. I am the understanding of those who understand and the splendor of the splendid.

**7.11** I am the strength of the strong, free from desire and passion. In beings, bull of the Bhāratas, I am the desire that does not oppose dharma.

**7.12** Whatever states of being there are, whether of sattva, rajas, or tamas, know that they come from me alone. But I am not in them; they are in me.

**7.13** Deluded by these states made of the three guṇas, this whole world does not recognize me, who am beyond them and imperishable.

**7.14** This divine māyā of mine, made of the guṇas, is hard to cross. Those who take refuge in me alone cross over this māyā.

**7.15** Evildoers do not take refuge in me: the deluded, the lowest of humans, whose knowledge is carried off by māyā and who have taken on a demonic nature.

**7.16** Four kinds of good people worship me, Arjuna: the distressed, the seeker of knowledge, the seeker of wealth, and the one who knows, bull of the Bhāratas.

**7.17** Of these the one who knows excels: always yoked and devoted to the One alone. I am exceedingly dear to the one who knows, and that one is dear to me.

**7.18** All of these are noble, but I regard the one who knows as my very self. With self yoked, that one abides in me alone as the highest goal.

**7.19** At the end of many births, the one who knows takes refuge in me, thinking "Vāsudeva is all." Such a great self is very rare.

**7.20** Those whose knowledge is carried off by various desires take refuge in other gods. They follow this or that rule, constrained by their own nature.

**7.21** Whatever form any devotee wishes to worship with faith, I make that very faith unshakable.

**7.22** Yoked with that faith, they seek to please that form. From it they obtain their desires, though in truth it is I alone who grant them.

**7.23** But the fruit for those of small understanding is finite. Those who sacrifice to the gods go to the gods; my devotees come to me.

**7.24** The unintelligent think that I, the unmanifest, have become manifest. They do not know my higher being, imperishable and supreme.

**7.25** I am not manifest to all, veiled as I am by my yoga-māyā. This deluded world does not recognize me, the unborn and imperishable.

**7.26** I know the beings that have passed, those that are present, and those yet to come, Arjuna. But no one knows me.

**7.27** All beings, scorcher of foes, fall into bewilderment at birth, through the delusion of the pairs of opposites, which arises from desire and aversion.

**7.28** But people of good deeds whose evil has come to an end are freed from the delusion of the pairs, and they worship me with firm vows.

**7.29** Those who take refuge in me and strive for release from old age and death know that brahman fully. They know the inner self [*adhyātma*] and action in its entirety.

**7.30** Those who know me together with what pertains to beings [*adhibhūta*], to the gods [*adhidaiva*], and to sacrifice [*adhiyajña*], with minds yoked, know me even at the time of death.

## Chapter 8 · *Akṣara-brahma-yoga* · The Yoga of the Imperishable Brahman

*Arjuna said:*

**8.1** What is that brahman? What is the inner self? What is action, Supreme Person? What is it that pertains to beings, and what is said to pertain to the gods?

**8.2** What pertains to sacrifice, and how is it here in this body, Madhusūdana? And how are you to be known at the time of death by those who are self-controlled?

*The Blessed Lord said:*

**8.3** Brahman is the supreme imperishable [*akṣara*]. Its own nature [*svabhāva*] is called the inner self. The creative outpouring that brings beings into existence is called action.

**8.4** What pertains to beings is perishable existence. What pertains to the gods is the Person [*puruṣa*]. What pertains to sacrifice is I myself, here in the body, best of the embodied.

**8.5** Whoever leaves the body at the time of death remembering me alone comes to my state of being. Of this there is no doubt.

**8.6** Whatever state of being one remembers when leaving the body at the end, to that state one goes, son of Kuntī, having always been shaped by it.

**8.7** Therefore remember me at all times, and fight. With mind and understanding fixed on me, you will come to me without doubt.

**8.8** With thought yoked by the yoga of practice, not straying elsewhere, meditating on the supreme divine Person, Pārtha, one goes to him.

**8.9** He is the seer, the ancient, the ruler, smaller than the minute, the upholder of all, of unthinkable form, sun-colored, beyond darkness.

**8.10** At the time of departure, one who meditates on him with unmoving mind, yoked with devotion and the power of yoga, and directs the breath fully between the eyebrows, reaches that supreme divine Person.

**8.11** Knowers of the Veda call it the imperishable. Ascetics free of passion enter it, and those who want it practice celibacy. I will tell you of that state briefly.

**8.12** Close all the gates of the body, confine the mind in the heart, place the breath in the head, and stay established in yogic concentration.

**8.13** Whoever departs this way, leaving the body while uttering Om, the one-syllabled brahman, and remembering me, goes to the supreme goal.

**8.14** I am easy to reach, Pārtha, for the yogin who is always yoked and remembers me constantly, never thinking of anything else.

**8.15** Having come to me, the great selves who have reached supreme perfection do not undergo birth again, that impermanent home of sorrow.

**8.16** All worlds up to the realm of Brahmā are subject to return, Arjuna. But one who comes to me, son of Kuntī, is not reborn.

**8.17** People who know that a day of Brahmā lasts a thousand ages [*yuga*], and that his night ends after a thousand ages, truly know day and night.

**8.18** At the coming of his day, all manifest things arise from the unmanifest. At the coming of his night, they dissolve into that same unmanifest.

**8.19** This same multitude of beings comes into being again and again. It dissolves helplessly at the coming of night, Pārtha, and arises again at the coming of day.

**8.20** But beyond that unmanifest is another, eternal unmanifest being, which does not perish when all beings perish.

**8.21** This unmanifest is called the imperishable, and they call it the supreme goal. Those who reach it do not return. That is my supreme abode.

**8.22** That supreme Person, Pārtha, in whom beings abide and by whom all this is pervaded, can be reached by exclusive devotion [*bhakti*].

**8.23** Now, bull of the Bhāratas, I will tell you the times at which departing yogins return or do not return.

**8.24** Fire, light, day, the bright half of the month, the six months when the sun moves north: those who know brahman and depart then go to brahman.

**8.25** Smoke, night, the dark half of the month, the six months when the sun moves south: the yogin who departs then reaches the light of the moon and returns.

**8.26** These two paths, the bright and the dark, are held to be eternal for the world. By one, a person goes and does not return; by the other, a person returns again.

**8.27** No yogin who knows these two paths is deluded, Pārtha. Therefore be yoked in yoga at all times, Arjuna.

**8.28** Merit and its fruit are assigned to the Vedas, to sacrifices, austerities, and gifts. Knowing this teaching, the yogin goes beyond all of it and reaches the supreme, primal place.

## Chapter 9 · *Rāja-vidyā-rāja-guhya-yoga* · The Yoga of the Royal Knowledge and the Royal Secret

*The Blessed Lord said:*

**9.1** Since you do not find fault, I will declare to you this most secret knowledge together with discernment. Knowing it, you will be freed from evil.

**9.2** It is royal knowledge, a royal secret [*rāja-vidyā, rāja-guhya*]: the supreme purifier, directly seen, in accord with dharma, very easy to practice, and imperishable.

**9.3** People without faith in this dharma, scorcher of foes, do not reach me. They return to the path of death and saṁsāra.

**9.4** This whole world is pervaded by me in my unmanifest form. All beings abide in me, but I do not abide in them.

**9.5** And yet beings do not abide in me. Behold my sovereign yoga! My self sustains beings and is their source, but it does not abide in them.

**9.6** The great wind moves everywhere yet always abides in space. In the same way, understand that all beings abide in me.

**9.7** At the end of a cosmic age [*kalpa*], son of Kuntī, all beings pass into my nature. At the beginning of a cosmic age I send them forth again.

**9.8** Resting on my own nature, I send forth again and again this whole multitude of beings, which is helpless under the power of nature.

**9.9** These actions do not bind me, Dhanañjaya. I sit as if indifferent, unattached to those actions.

**9.10** With me as overseer, nature gives birth to the moving and the unmoving. For this reason, son of Kuntī, the world revolves.

**9.11** The deluded despise me when I take on a human body. They do not know my higher being as the great lord of beings.

**9.12** Their hopes, their actions, and their knowledge are vain, and they lack insight. They take on the deluding nature of fiends and demons.

**9.13** But the great selves, Pārtha, rely on the divine nature. Knowing me as the imperishable source of beings, they worship me with minds that do not stray.

**9.14** Always glorifying me, striving with firm vows, bowing to me with devotion, always yoked, they worship me.

**9.15** Others worship me by offering the sacrifice of knowledge. They worship me as one, as distinct, and as manifold, facing in all directions.

**9.16** I am the ritual; I am the sacrifice; I am the offering to the ancestors; I am the healing herb. I am the mantra; I am the clarified butter; I am the fire; I am the oblation.

**9.17** I am the father of this world, its mother, its establisher, its grandfather. I am what is to be known, the purifier, the syllable Om, the Ṛg, the Sāman, and the Yajus.

**9.18** I am the goal, the supporter, the lord, the witness, the dwelling, the refuge, the friend. I am the origin, the dissolution, the ground, the treasure-house, the imperishable seed.

**9.19** I give heat; I hold back the rain and send it forth. I am immortality and death, being and non-being [*sat* and *asat*], Arjuna.

**9.20** Knowers of the three Vedas, drinkers of soma purified of evil, worship me with sacrifices and pray for the way to heaven. They reach the pure world of the lord of the gods and enjoy in heaven the divine enjoyments of the gods.

**9.21** Having enjoyed that vast heavenly world, they enter the world of mortals again when their merit is used up. So those who follow the dharma of the three Vedas, desiring desires, gain only coming and going.

**9.22** But some people think of no other and worship me alone. They are always yoked, and for them I secure both gain and safekeeping [*yoga-kṣema*].

**9.23** Even those who are devoted to other gods and sacrifice to them with faith are in fact sacrificing to me, son of Kuntī, though not according to the rule.

**9.24** For I am the enjoyer and the lord of all sacrifices. But they do not recognize me in truth, and so they fall.

**9.25** Those vowed to the gods go to the gods; those vowed to the ancestors go to the ancestors; those who sacrifice to spirits go to the spirits. Those who sacrifice to me come to me.

**9.26** Whoever offers me with devotion a leaf, a flower, a fruit, or water, I accept that offering of devotion from one whose self is pure.

**9.27** Whatever you do, whatever you eat, whatever you offer, whatever you give, whatever austerity you perform, son of Kuntī, do it as an offering to me.

**9.28** In this way you will be freed from the bonds of action and their good and evil fruits. With your self yoked in the yoga of renunciation, you will be freed and come to me.

**9.29** I am the same toward all beings. No one is hateful to me, and no one is dear. But those who worship me with devotion are in me, and I am in them.

**9.30** Even if someone of very bad conduct worships me with undivided devotion, that person should be considered good, for he has resolved rightly.

**9.31** He quickly becomes a self of dharma and reaches lasting peace. Son of Kuntī, know for certain: my devotee does not perish.

**9.32** For those who take refuge in me, Pārtha, even those of lowly birth [*pāpa-yonayaḥ*], and women, vaiśyas, and śūdras too, reach the supreme goal.
> *Note:* The Sanskrit can be read in two ways. One makes "of lowly birth" a separate group. The other makes it a label for the women, vaiśyas, and śūdras that follow. Many modern readers take the first, more inclusive reading.

**9.33** How much more, then, pure brāhmaṇas and devoted royal seers! You have come into this impermanent, joyless world, so worship me.

**9.34** Let your mind be on me; be devoted to me; sacrifice to me; bow down to me. Having yoked your self in this way, with me as your highest aim, you will come to me.

## Chapter 10 · *Vibhūti-yoga* · The Yoga of Divine Manifestations

*The Blessed Lord said:*

**10.1** Once again, mighty-armed one, hear my supreme word. You take delight in it, and I will speak it to you because I wish for your good.

**10.2** Neither the hosts of gods nor the great seers know my origin, for in every way I am the source of the gods and the great seers.

**10.3** One who knows me as unborn and without beginning, the great lord of the world, is undeluded among mortals and freed from all evils.

**10.4** Understanding, knowledge, freedom from delusion, patience, truth, self-restraint, calm, pleasure and pain, existence and non-existence, fear and fearlessness,

**10.5** non-harming [*ahiṁsā*], equanimity, contentment, austerity, generosity, fame and infamy: these various states of beings come from me alone.

**10.6** The seven great seers of old and the four Manus came from my mind and share my nature. From them come these creatures in the world.

**10.7** One who knows in truth this extended power [*vibhūti*] and yoga of mine is yoked by unwavering yoga. Of this there is no doubt.

**10.8** I am the origin of all, and from me everything proceeds. Knowing this, the wise worship me, filled with love [*bhāva*].

**10.9** With their thoughts on me and their lives given to me, they enlighten one another and always speak of me. They are content and full of delight.

**10.10** To those who are always yoked and worship me with love, I give the yoga of understanding by which they come to me.

**10.11** Out of compassion for them, abiding in their own being, I destroy the darkness born of ignorance with the shining lamp of knowledge.

*Arjuna said:*

**10.12** You are the supreme brahman, the supreme abode, the supreme purifier: the eternal divine Person, the first god, unborn and all-pervading.

**10.13** So all the seers say of you, and the divine seer Nārada, Asita Devala, and Vyāsa. And you yourself tell me so.

**10.14** I hold all that you tell me to be true, Keśava. Neither the gods nor the demons, Blessed One, know your manifestation.

**10.15** You alone know yourself by yourself, Supreme Person: source of beings, lord of beings, god of gods, lord of the world.

**10.16** Tell me fully of your divine extended powers, by which you pervade these worlds and abide in them.

**10.17** How may I know you, yogin, through constant meditation? In what forms of being should I meditate on you, Blessed One?

**10.18** Tell me again in detail of your yoga and extended power, Janārdana. I never tire of hearing your nectar.

*The Blessed Lord said:*

**10.19** Very well. I will tell you of my divine extended powers, best of Kurus, but only the principal ones, for there is no end to my extent.

**10.20** I am the self, Guḍākeśa, abiding in the heart of all beings. I am the beginning, the middle, and the end of beings.

**10.21** Among the Ādityas I am Viṣṇu; among lights, the radiant sun. I am Marīci among the Maruts, and among the stars I am the moon.

**10.22** Among the Vedas I am the Sāmaveda; among the gods, Indra. Among the senses I am the mind, and in beings I am consciousness [*cetanā*].

**10.23** Among the Rudras I am Śaṅkara; among yakṣas and rākṣasas, Kubera, lord of wealth. Among the Vasus I am fire, and among mountains, Meru.

**10.24** Among household priests, Pārtha, know me as the chief, Bṛhaspati. Among generals I am Skanda; among bodies of water, the ocean.

**10.25** Among the great seers I am Bhṛgu; among utterances, the single syllable [Om]. Among sacrifices I am the sacrifice of murmured prayer [*japa*]; among immovable things, the Himālaya.

**10.26** Among all trees I am the aśvattha [sacred fig]; among divine seers, Nārada. Among gandharvas I am Citraratha; among the perfected, the sage Kapila.

**10.27** Among horses know me as Uccaiḥśravas, born of the nectar; among lordly elephants, Airāvata; and among men, the king.

**10.28** Among weapons I am the thunderbolt; among cows, the wish-granting cow. I am Kandarpa [Love], who begets; among serpents I am Vāsuki.

**10.29** Among nāgas I am Ananta; among water-beings, Varuṇa. Among the ancestors I am Aryaman; among those who restrain, Yama.

**10.30** Among the daityas I am Prahlāda; among those who reckon, I am time. Among beasts I am the lion; among birds, Garuḍa, son of Vinatā.

**10.31** Among purifiers I am the wind; among those who bear arms, Rāma. Among sea-creatures I am the makara; among rivers, the Gaṅgā.

**10.32** Of creations I am the beginning, the end, and the middle, Arjuna. Among sciences I am the science of the self; in those who debate, I am the reasoning.

**10.33** Among letters I am the letter A; among compounds, the copulative compound [*dvandva*]. I am imperishable time; I am the establisher, facing in all directions.

**10.34** I am all-seizing death and the origin of all that is to come. Among feminine powers I am fame, fortune, speech, memory, intelligence, steadfastness, and patience.
> *Note:* These seven nouns are feminine in Sanskrit, and each is also a goddess.

**10.35** Among chants I am the Bṛhat-sāman; among meters, the gāyatrī. Among months I am Mārgaśīrṣa; among seasons, the flowering spring.

**10.36** I am the gambling of cheats and the splendor of the splendid. I am victory, I am resolve, I am the goodness [*sattva*] of the good.

**10.37** Among the Vṛṣṇis I am Vāsudeva; among the Pāṇḍavas, Dhanañjaya. Among sages I am Vyāsa; among poets, the poet Uśanas.

**10.38** I am the rod of those who punish and the statecraft of those who seek victory. I am the silence of secret things and the knowledge of those who know.

**10.39** Whatever is the seed of all beings, that I am, Arjuna. No being, moving or unmoving, could exist without me.

**10.40** There is no end to my divine extended powers, scorcher of foes. What I have said of their extent is only a set of examples.

**10.41** Whatever being has power, splendor, or vigor, understand that it arises from a fraction of my brilliance.

**10.42** But what use is all this detailed knowledge to you, Arjuna? I stand supporting this whole world with a single fraction of myself.

## Chapter 11 · *Viśva-rūpa-darśana-yoga* · The Yoga of the Vision of the Universal Form

*Arjuna said:*

**11.1** As a favor to me, you have spoken the supreme secret called the inner self [*adhyātma*]. Through these words of yours my delusion is gone.

**11.2** For I have heard from you in detail, lotus-eyed one, of the arising and passing away of beings, and of your imperishable greatness.

**11.3** It is just as you have described yourself, supreme lord. Now, Supreme Person, I wish to see your sovereign form.

**11.4** If you think it possible for me to see it, Lord, lord of yoga, then show me your imperishable self.

*The Blessed Lord said:*

**11.5** Behold, Pārtha, my forms by the hundreds and thousands: various, divine, of many colors and shapes.

**11.6** Behold the Ādityas, the Vasus, the Rudras, the two Aśvins, and the Maruts. Behold many wonders never seen before, Bhārata.

**11.7** Behold today the whole world, moving and unmoving, standing as one here in my body, Guḍākeśa, and whatever else you wish to see.

**11.8** But you cannot see me with this eye of yours. I give you a divine eye. Behold my sovereign yoga!

*Sañjaya said:*

**11.9** Having spoken thus, O king, Hari, the great lord of yoga, showed Pārtha his supreme sovereign form.

**11.10** It had many mouths and eyes and many wondrous sights, many divine ornaments and many divine weapons raised.

**11.11** It wore divine garlands and garments and was anointed with divine perfumes: the infinite god, all-wondrous, facing in all directions.

**11.12** If the light of a thousand suns were to rise at once in the sky, it might be like the light of that great self.

**11.13** There, in the body of the god of gods, Pāṇḍava saw the whole world, divided in many ways, standing as one.

**11.14** Then Dhanañjaya, filled with amazement and with his hair standing on end, bowed his head to the god, joined his palms, and said:

*Arjuna said:*

**11.15** O God, I see in your body the gods and all the hosts of beings: Brahmā the lord, seated on the lotus, and all the seers and divine serpents.

**11.16** I see you on every side, endless in form, with countless arms, bellies, mouths, and eyes. I see no end of you, no middle, and no beginning, lord of all, whose form is the universe.

**11.17** I see you wearing a crown and bearing a mace and discus, a mass of brilliance shining everywhere. You are hard to look at, blazing on all sides like burning fire and sun, beyond measure.

**11.18** You are the imperishable, the supreme thing to be known. You are the final resting place of all this. You are the undying guardian of eternal dharma. You are the ancient Person, I believe.

**11.19** I see you without beginning, middle, or end, of infinite power and countless arms, with moon and sun as eyes and blazing fire as mouth, burning this whole world with your radiance.

**11.20** The space between heaven and earth and all the directions are filled by you alone. Seeing this wondrous and terrible form of yours, great self, the three worlds tremble.

**11.21** These hosts of gods enter into you. Some, terrified, praise you with joined palms. Crying "Hail!", the hosts of great seers and the perfected praise you with abundant hymns.

**11.22** The Rudras, Ādityas, Vasus, and Sādhyas, the Viśvedevas, the two Aśvins, the Maruts, and the ancestors, and the hosts of gandharvas, yakṣas, asuras, and the perfected all gaze at you in amazement.

**11.23** Your form is great, mighty-armed one, with many mouths and eyes, many arms, thighs, and feet, many bellies, terrible with many tusks. Seeing it, the worlds tremble, and so do I.

**11.24** You touch the sky, blazing and many-colored, with gaping mouths and huge blazing eyes. Seeing you, Viṣṇu, my innermost self trembles, and I find no steadiness or peace.

**11.25** Seeing your mouths, terrible with tusks, like the fire of time [*kāla*] at the world's end, I lose all sense of direction and find no refuge. Be gracious, lord of gods, home of the world!

**11.26** And all these sons of Dhṛtarāṣṭra, together with hosts of kings, Bhīṣma, Droṇa, and that son of a charioteer [Karṇa], together with our own chief warriors,

**11.27** are rushing into your terrible mouths with their fearsome tusks. Some are seen caught between your teeth, their heads crushed.

**11.28** As the many rushing torrents of rivers race toward the ocean, so these heroes of the human world enter your blazing mouths.

**11.29** As moths rush with great speed into a blazing fire to their destruction, so these worlds rush with great speed into your mouths to their destruction.

**11.30** Devouring all the worlds on every side with your flaming mouths, you lick your lips. Your terrible rays fill the whole world with brilliance and scorch it, Viṣṇu.

**11.31** Tell me who you are, with so terrible a form. Homage to you, best of gods; be gracious! I want to understand you, the primal one, for I do not understand what you are doing.

*The Blessed Lord said:*

**11.32** I am time [*kāla*], the mighty destroyer of worlds, set in motion here to annihilate the worlds. Even without you, all these warriors drawn up in the opposing ranks will cease to be.

**11.33** Therefore stand up and win glory. Conquer your enemies and enjoy a prosperous kingdom. I have already slain them. Be merely the instrument [*nimitta-mātra*], left-handed archer.

**11.34** Droṇa, Bhīṣma, Jayadratha, Karṇa, and the other heroic warriors are already slain by me. Kill them, and do not waver. Fight! You will conquer your rivals in battle.

*Sañjaya said:*

**11.35** Hearing these words of Keśava, the crowned one [Arjuna], trembling, joined his palms and bowed down. Overwhelmed with fear, he bowed again and spoke to Kṛṣṇa in a faltering voice:

*Arjuna said:*

**11.36** It is right, Hṛṣīkeśa, that the world rejoices and delights in praising you. The demons flee in terror in all directions, and all the hosts of the perfected bow down to you.

**11.37** And why should they not bow to you, great self? You are greater even than Brahmā, the first creator. Infinite lord of gods, home of the world, you are the imperishable: being and non-being, and what is beyond both.

**11.38** You are the first god, the ancient Person, the supreme resting place of this world. You are the knower and what is to be known, and the supreme abode. The world is pervaded by you, whose form is infinite.

**11.39** You are Vāyu, Yama, Agni, Varuṇa, and the moon; you are Prajāpati and the great-grandfather. Homage, homage to you a thousand times, and again and yet again homage to you!

**11.40** Homage to you in front and behind, homage to you on every side, you who are all. Your valor is infinite and your might is immeasurable. You encompass everything, so you are everything.

**11.41** I thought of you as a friend, and spoke rashly: "O Kṛṣṇa, O Yādava, O friend." I did not know this greatness of yours, and spoke from carelessness or from affection.

**11.42** If I showed you disrespect in jest, at play, lying down, sitting, or eating, whether alone or before others, Acyuta, I ask forgiveness of you, the immeasurable one.

**11.43** You are the father of the world, of all that moves and does not move. You are its object of worship and its most venerable teacher. No one equals you in the three worlds; how could anyone surpass you, whose power is beyond compare?

**11.44** So I bow down and lay my body before you, and seek your grace, praiseworthy Lord. Bear with me, O God, as a father with a son, a friend with a friend, a lover with the beloved.

**11.45** I am thrilled to have seen what was never seen before, yet my mind is shaken with fear. Show me that other form of yours, O God. Be gracious, lord of gods, home of the world.

**11.46** I want to see you as before, wearing your crown, with mace and discus in hand. Take on that four-armed form, thousand-armed one, whose form is the universe.

*The Blessed Lord said:*

**11.47** By my grace, Arjuna, through my own yoga, I have shown you this supreme form. It is made of brilliance, universal, infinite, and primal, and no one but you has seen it before.

**11.48** Not through the Vedas, sacrifices, or study, not through gifts or rites or fierce austerities, can anyone but you in the human world see me in this form, hero of the Kurus.

**11.49** Do not be afraid or bewildered at seeing this terrible form of mine. Free of fear and with a glad mind, behold again this form of mine.

*Sañjaya said:*

**11.50** Having spoken thus to Arjuna, Vāsudeva showed his own form again. Taking on his gentle form once more, the great self consoled the frightened Arjuna.

*Arjuna said:*

**11.51** Seeing this gentle human form of yours, Janārdana, I am now composed and have returned to my own nature.

*The Blessed Lord said:*

**11.52** This form of mine that you have seen is very hard to see. Even the gods always long to see it.

**11.53** Not through the Vedas, austerity, gifts, or sacrifice can I be seen as you have seen me.

**11.54** But through undivided devotion, Arjuna, I can be known in this way, seen in truth, and entered, scorcher of foes.

**11.55** One who does my work, who holds me as highest, who is devoted to me, free of attachment and without hostility toward any being, comes to me, Pāṇḍava.

## Chapter 12 · *Bhakti-yoga* · The Yoga of Devotion

*Arjuna said:*

**12.1** Some devotees, always yoked, worship you in this way. Others worship the imperishable, the unmanifest. Which of these know yoga better?

*The Blessed Lord said:*

**12.2** Those who fix their minds on me and worship me, always yoked and endowed with supreme faith, I consider the most fully yoked.

**12.3** But there are also those who worship the imperishable, which cannot be defined: the unmanifest, all-pervading and unthinkable, unchanging [*kūṭa-stha*], unmoving and constant.

**12.4** They restrain all the senses, are equal-minded toward everything, and delight in the welfare of all beings. They too reach me.

**12.5** The struggle is greater for those whose thought is fixed on the unmanifest, for the goal of the unmanifest is hard for the embodied to reach.

**12.6** But others surrender all actions in me and are intent on me. They meditate on me and worship me with undivided yoga.

**12.7** Their thought has entered into me, Pārtha, and I soon become their deliverer from the ocean of death and saṁsāra.

**12.8** Place your mind in me alone, and let your understanding enter into me. From then on you will dwell in me alone. Of this there is no doubt.

**12.9** But if you cannot fix your thought firmly on me, then seek to reach me through the yoga of practice, Dhanañjaya.

**12.10** If you are not capable even of practice, devote yourself to doing work for my sake. Even by doing actions for my sake you will reach perfection.

**12.11** If you cannot do even this, then rely on my yoga, control yourself, and give up the fruit of all actions.

**12.12** Knowledge is better than practice. Meditation is better than knowledge. Giving up the fruit of action is better than meditation, and from giving it up, peace follows at once.

**12.13** One who hates no being, who is friendly and compassionate, without "mine" and without I-making, the same in pain and pleasure, and patient,

**12.14** always content, a yogin, self-controlled and firm in resolve, with mind and understanding offered to me: that devotee of mine is dear to me.

**12.15** One from whom the world does not shrink and who does not shrink from the world, free from elation, impatience, fear, and agitation, is dear to me.

**12.16** One who expects nothing, who is pure and capable, impartial and free of anxiety, who has given up all ambitious undertakings: that devotee of mine is dear to me.

**12.17** One who neither rejoices nor hates, neither grieves nor desires, who has given up both good and bad and is full of devotion, is dear to me.

**12.18** One who is the same toward enemy and friend, in honor and dishonor, the same in cold and heat, pleasure and pain, free from attachment,

**12.19** who treats blame and praise alike, who is silent and content with whatever comes, without a fixed home, steady-minded, and full of devotion: that person is dear to me.

**12.20** But those devotees who, full of faith and holding me as highest, follow this nectar of dharma as I have taught it are exceedingly dear to me.

## Chapter 13 · *Kṣetra-kṣetrajña-vibhāga-yoga* · The Yoga of the Field and the Knower of the Field

*The Blessed Lord said:*

**13.1** This body, son of Kuntī, is called the field [*kṣetra*]. Those who know it call the one who knows it the knower of the field [*kṣetrajña*].

**13.2** Know that I am the knower of the field in all fields, Bhārata. Knowledge of the field and of the knower of the field is what I hold to be true knowledge.

**13.3** Hear from me briefly what the field is and what it is like, what its changes are and where it comes from, and who the knower is and what powers he has.

**13.4** Seers have sung of this in many ways and in various distinct hymns, and in the reasoned, conclusive words of the Brahma-sūtras.

**13.5** The great elements, I-making, understanding, and the unmanifest; the ten senses and the one [the mind]; the five domains of the senses;

**13.6** desire, aversion, pleasure, pain, the bodily whole, awareness, and steadfastness: this, in brief, is the field with its changes.

**13.7** Absence of pride, absence of deceit, non-harming, patience, uprightness, service to the teacher, purity, steadiness, self-restraint;

**13.8** dispassion toward sense objects, absence of I-making, seeing clearly the evil of birth, death, old age, sickness, and pain;

**13.9** non-attachment, not clinging to son, wife, home, and the like, and constant evenness of mind whether the wanted or the unwanted comes;

**13.10** unwavering devotion to me through undivided yoga, resort to solitary places, distaste for crowds;

**13.11** constancy in knowledge of the inner self, and insight into the aim of the knowledge of truth: this is called knowledge. What is contrary to it is ignorance.

**13.12** I will declare what is to be known, knowing which one reaches immortality: the beginningless supreme brahman, which is said to be neither being nor non-being.

**13.13** With hands and feet everywhere, with eyes, heads, and mouths everywhere, with ears everywhere, it stands enveloping everything in the world.

**13.14** It seems to have the qualities of all the senses, yet it is free of all the senses. It is unattached, yet it supports all. It is free of the guṇas, yet it experiences the guṇas.

**13.15** It is outside and inside beings, unmoving and moving. It is too subtle to be known. It is far away, and it is near.

**13.16** It is undivided, yet it seems to be divided among beings. It is to be known as the one who sustains beings, devours them, and brings them forth.

**13.17** It is the light of lights, said to be beyond darkness. It is knowledge, what is to be known, and what knowledge reaches, seated in the heart of all.

**13.18** So the field, knowledge, and what is to be known have been briefly described. My devotee who understands this enters into my state of being.

**13.19** Know that nature [*prakṛti*] and person [*puruṣa*] are both beginningless, and that the changes and the guṇas arise from nature.

**13.20** Nature is said to be the cause of effect, instrument, and agency. The person is said to be the cause of the experience of pleasure and pain.

**13.21** For the person, situated in nature, experiences the guṇas born of nature. Attachment to the guṇas causes its births in good and evil wombs.

**13.22** The supreme person in this body is called the witness, the one who consents, the supporter, the experiencer, the great lord, and the supreme self.

**13.23** One who knows the person, and nature together with the guṇas, in this way is not born again, however one may live.

**13.24** Some see the self in the self by the self through meditation; others through the yoga of Sāṅkhya; and others through the yoga of action.

**13.25** Yet others, not knowing this, hear it from others and worship. Devoted to what they have heard, they too cross beyond death.

**13.26** Whatever being comes into existence, moving or unmoving, know, bull of the Bhāratas, that it arises from the union of the field and the knower of the field.

**13.27** One who sees the supreme lord abiding equally in all beings, not perishing when they perish, truly sees.

**13.28** Seeing the same lord established everywhere, one does not harm the self by the self, and so goes to the supreme goal.

**13.29** One who sees that all actions are done by nature alone, and that the self is therefore not the doer, truly sees.

**13.30** When one sees the separate existence of beings as resting in the One, and their unfolding from that One alone, one reaches brahman.

**13.31** Because it is beginningless and free of the guṇas, this imperishable supreme self neither acts nor is tainted, son of Kuntī, even though it dwells in the body.

**13.32** Space pervades everything yet is not tainted, because it is so subtle. In the same way the self, dwelling everywhere in the body, is not tainted.

**13.33** As the one sun lights up this whole world, so the lord of the field lights up the whole field, Bhārata.

**13.34** Those who, with the eye of knowledge, know this difference between the field and the knower of the field, and know the release of beings from nature, go to the supreme.

> *Note:* Some manuscripts open this chapter with an extra verse (Arjuna's question about nature, person, field, and knower), which would make it 35 verses. The standard numbering, used here, leaves that verse out.

---

## Chapter 14 · *Guṇa-traya-vibhāga-yoga* · The Yoga of the Three Guṇas

*The Blessed Lord said:*

**14.1** I will declare again the supreme knowledge, the highest of all knowledge. Knowing it, all the sages have gone from here to supreme perfection.

**14.2** Relying on this knowledge, they have come to share my nature. They are not born at the creation of the world, and they are not disturbed at its dissolution.

**14.3** My womb is the great brahman [here, primal nature]. In it I place the seed, and from that, Bhārata, all beings come to be.

**14.4** Whatever forms arise in any womb, son of Kuntī, the great brahman is their womb, and I am the father who gives the seed.

**14.5** Sattva, rajas, and tamas, the guṇas born of nature, bind the imperishable embodied one in the body, mighty-armed one.

**14.6** Of these, sattva is stainless, so it gives light and is free of disorder. It binds by attachment to happiness and attachment to knowledge, sinless one.

**14.7** Know that rajas is passion in nature, arising from craving and attachment. It binds the embodied one, son of Kuntī, by attachment to action.

**14.8** But know that tamas is born of ignorance and deludes all embodied beings. It binds by negligence, laziness, and sleep, Bhārata.

**14.9** Sattva attaches one to happiness and rajas to action, Bhārata. But tamas covers knowledge and attaches one to negligence.

**14.10** Sattva arises by overpowering rajas and tamas, Bhārata. Rajas arises by overpowering sattva and tamas. In the same way, tamas arises by overpowering sattva and rajas.

**14.11** When the light of knowledge shines through all the gates of this body, then know that sattva has grown strong.

**14.12** Greed, activity, the undertaking of actions, restlessness, and longing arise when rajas has grown strong, bull of the Bhāratas.

**14.13** Darkness, inactivity, negligence, and delusion arise when tamas has grown strong, joy of the Kurus.

**14.14** When the embodied one dies while sattva prevails, it reaches the stainless worlds of those who know the highest.

**14.15** Dying in rajas, one is born among those attached to action. Dying in tamas, one is born in the wombs of the deluded.

**14.16** The fruit of good action is said to be pure and of the nature of sattva. The fruit of rajas is pain, and the fruit of tamas is ignorance.

**14.17** Knowledge arises from sattva and greed from rajas. Negligence and delusion arise from tamas, and ignorance too.

**14.18** Those established in sattva go upward. Those of rajas stay in the middle. Those of tamas, living by the lowest guṇa, go downward.

**14.19** When the seer sees no doer other than the guṇas and knows what is beyond the guṇas, he comes to my state of being.

**14.20** Going beyond these three guṇas, from which the body arises, the embodied one is freed from birth, death, old age, and sorrow, and reaches immortality.

*Arjuna said:*

**14.21** By what marks, Lord, is one known who has gone beyond these three guṇas? How does that person live, and how does he go beyond the three guṇas?

*The Blessed Lord said:*

**14.22** One who does not hate light, activity, or delusion when they arise, Pāṇḍava, and does not long for them when they cease;

**14.23** who sits as if indifferent and is not disturbed by the guṇas; who knows "it is only the guṇas moving" and stands firm without wavering;

**14.24** who is the same in pain and pleasure, abiding in the self, to whom a clod, a stone, and gold are the same; who is equal toward the pleasant and the unpleasant, steadfast, equal toward blame and praise;

**14.25** who is equal in honor and dishonor, equal toward friend and foe, and has given up all ambitious undertakings: that one is said to have gone beyond the guṇas.

**14.26** And one who serves me with the unwavering yoga of devotion goes beyond these guṇas and is fit to become brahman.

**14.27** For I am the foundation [*pratiṣṭhā*] of brahman, of the immortal and imperishable, of eternal dharma, and of absolute happiness.

## Chapter 15 · *Puruṣottama-yoga* · The Yoga of the Supreme Person

*The Blessed Lord said:*

**15.1** They speak of an imperishable aśvattha tree with its roots above and its branches below. Its leaves are the Vedic hymns. One who knows it knows the Veda.

**15.2** Its branches spread below and above, nourished by the guṇas, with sense objects as their shoots. And below, its roots stretch out, binding people to action in the human world.

**15.3** Its form cannot be perceived here, nor its end, nor its beginning, nor its foundation. Cut down this firmly rooted aśvattha with the strong axe of non-attachment.

**15.4** Then seek that place from which those who go there never return, thinking: "I take refuge in that primal Person from whom the ancient stream of activity flowed forth."

**15.5** Without pride or delusion, having conquered the fault of attachment, always devoted to the inner self, with desires turned away and freed from the pairs called pleasure and pain, the undeluded go to that imperishable place.

**15.6** Neither sun nor moon nor fire lights it. Those who go there do not return. That is my supreme abode.

**15.7** A fragment of me, becoming an eternal living being [*jīva*] in the world of the living, draws to itself the senses, with the mind as the sixth, which rest in nature.

**15.8** When the lord takes on a body and when he leaves it, he takes these with him and goes, as the wind carries scents from their sources.

**15.9** Presiding over hearing, sight, touch, taste, and smell, and over the mind, he enjoys the objects of the senses.

**15.10** The deluded do not see him departing, staying, or enjoying, joined with the guṇas. Those with the eye of knowledge see him.

**15.11** Yogins who strive see him established in themselves. But those whose selves are unformed and who lack insight do not see him, even when they strive.

**15.12** The brilliance in the sun that lights up the whole world, the brilliance in the moon and in fire: know that this brilliance is mine.

**15.13** Entering the earth, I sustain beings with my vigor. Becoming soma, the essence of sap, I nourish all plants.

**15.14** Becoming the digestive fire, I dwell in the bodies of living beings. Joined with the in-breath and out-breath, I digest the four kinds of food.

**15.15** I am seated in the heart of all. From me come memory and knowledge, and their loss. I am what is to be known through all the Vedas. I am the maker of the Vedānta, and I am the knower of the Veda.

**15.16** There are two persons in this world: the perishable [*kṣara*] and the imperishable [*akṣara*]. The perishable is all beings. The imperishable is called the unchanging one [*kūṭa-stha*].

**15.17** But there is another, the highest person, called the supreme self. As the imperishable lord, he enters the three worlds and sustains them.

**15.18** Because I am beyond the perishable and higher even than the imperishable, I am celebrated in the world and in the Veda as the Supreme Person [*puruṣottama*].

**15.19** One who is undeluded and knows me in this way as the Supreme Person knows all, and worships me with one's whole being, Bhārata.

**15.20** So I have declared this most secret teaching, sinless one. Understanding it, one becomes wise and has done all that needs to be done, Bhārata.

---

## Chapter 16 · *Daivāsura-sampad-vibhāga-yoga* · The Yoga of the Divine and Demonic Endowments

*The Blessed Lord said:*

**16.1** Fearlessness, purity of being, steadfastness in the yoga of knowledge, generosity, self-restraint, sacrifice, study of scripture, austerity, uprightness;

**16.2** non-harming, truth, freedom from anger, renunciation, peace, refraining from slander, compassion for beings, freedom from greed, gentleness, modesty, steadiness;

**16.3** vigor, forgiveness, fortitude, purity, freedom from malice, and freedom from arrogance: these belong to one born to the divine endowment [*daivī sampad*], Bhārata.

**16.4** Hypocrisy, arrogance, conceit, anger, harshness, and ignorance belong to one born to the demonic endowment [*āsurī sampad*], Pārtha.

**16.5** The divine endowment is held to lead to liberation, and the demonic to bondage. Do not grieve, Pāṇḍava. You are born to the divine endowment.

**16.6** There are two kinds of beings created in this world, the divine and the demonic. I have described the divine at length. Now hear from me about the demonic, Pārtha.

**16.7** Demonic people do not know when to act and when to refrain. Neither purity nor good conduct nor truth is found in them.

**16.8** They say the world is without truth, without foundation, and without a lord. They say it does not come from any chain of causes, but from desire alone. What else, they ask, could cause it?

**16.9** Holding this view, these lost selves of small understanding and cruel deeds rise up as enemies to destroy the world.

**16.10** Relying on desire that can never be satisfied, full of hypocrisy, pride, and intoxication, they take up false notions through delusion and act on impure vows.

**16.11** They cling to boundless anxiety that ends only with death. They hold the enjoyment of desires as the highest aim and are sure that this is all there is.

**16.12** Bound by hundreds of ties of hope and given over to desire and anger, they seek to pile up wealth by unjust means to enjoy their desires.

**16.13** "This I have gained today; this wish I will fulfill. This is mine, and this wealth too will be mine.

**16.14** I have killed that enemy, and I will kill others too. I am the lord, I am the enjoyer. I am successful, strong, and happy.

**16.15** I am rich and well-born. Who else is like me? I will sacrifice, I will give, I will rejoice." So they think, deluded by ignorance.

**16.16** Bewildered by many thoughts, caught in the net of delusion, attached to the enjoyment of desires, they fall into a foul hell.

**16.17** Full of self-regard and stubborn, filled with the pride and intoxication of wealth, they offer sacrifices in name only, with hypocrisy and not according to the rule.

**16.18** Given to I-making, force, arrogance, desire, and anger, these envious ones hate me, who dwell in their own bodies and in the bodies of others.

**16.19** These hateful, cruel, vile, inauspicious lowest of humans I hurl again and again, through the cycles of rebirth, into demonic wombs.

**16.20** Entering demonic wombs, deluded birth after birth and never reaching me, son of Kuntī, they go to the lowest state.

**16.21** This is the threefold gate of hell, which destroys the self: desire, anger, and greed. Therefore one should give up these three.

**16.22** A person released from these three gates of darkness, son of Kuntī, does what is good for the self and then goes to the supreme goal.

**16.23** One who casts aside the rule of scripture [*śāstra*] and acts on the impulse of desire reaches neither perfection, nor happiness, nor the supreme goal.

**16.24** So let scripture be your standard in deciding what should and should not be done. Knowing what the rule of scripture says, you should do your work here.

## Chapter 17 · *Śraddhā-traya-vibhāga-yoga* · The Yoga of the Three Kinds of Faith

*Arjuna said:*

**17.1** Some people set aside the rule of scripture yet sacrifice with faith. What is their standing, Kṛṣṇa? Is it sattva, rajas, or tamas?

*The Blessed Lord said:*

**17.2** The faith of embodied beings is of three kinds, born of their own nature: of sattva, of rajas, and of tamas. Hear about it.

**17.3** Each person's faith follows his inner being [*sattva*], Bhārata. A person is made of faith: whatever one's faith is, that is what one is.

**17.4** People of sattva worship the gods. People of rajas worship yakṣas and rākṣasas. The others, people of tamas, worship ghosts and hosts of spirits.

**17.5** Some people practice terrible austerities that scripture does not prescribe. They are full of hypocrisy and I-making, driven by the force of desire and passion.

**17.6** Senseless, they torment the elements that make up the body, and me as well, who dwell within the body. Know that their resolve is demonic.

**17.7** The food that each person likes is also of three kinds, and so are sacrifice, austerity, and giving. Hear how they differ.

**17.8** Foods that increase life, vitality, strength, health, happiness, and satisfaction, foods that are savory, smooth, nourishing, and pleasing to the heart, are dear to people of sattva.

**17.9** Foods that are bitter, sour, salty, very hot, pungent, harsh, and burning, and that cause pain, grief, and sickness, are desired by people of rajas.

**17.10** Food that is stale, tasteless, putrid, and spoiled, leftover and impure, is dear to people of tamas.

**17.11** Sacrifice is of sattva when it is offered according to the rule by those who do not want its fruit, with the mind fixed only on the thought "this ought to be offered."

**17.12** But sacrifice offered with an eye to its fruit, or for show, best of Bhāratas, know to be of rajas.

**17.13** Sacrifice that ignores the rule, in which no food is shared, no mantras are spoken, no priests are paid, and there is no faith, is called of tamas.

**17.14** Reverence for the gods, the twice-born, teachers, and the wise; purity, uprightness, celibacy, and non-harming are called austerity of the body.

**17.15** Speech that does not cause distress, that is truthful, pleasant, and beneficial, and regular recitation of scripture are called austerity of speech.

**17.16** Calm of mind, gentleness, silence, self-restraint, and purity of being are called austerity of the mind.

**17.17** When yoked people practice this threefold austerity with supreme faith and without wanting its fruit, it is called of sattva.

**17.18** Austerity practiced to gain respect, honor, and reverence, or out of hypocrisy, is called of rajas. It is unstable and does not last.

**17.19** Austerity practiced from a deluded idea, by torturing oneself, or to ruin another, is called of tamas.

**17.20** A gift is held to be of sattva when it is given because it ought to be given, to one who cannot repay, at the right place and time, to a worthy person.

**17.21** But a gift given to get something back, or with an eye to its fruit, or given grudgingly, is held to be of rajas.

**17.22** A gift given at the wrong place and time, to the unworthy, without respect and with contempt, is called of tamas.

**17.23** "Om tat sat": this is held to be the threefold name of brahman. By it, the brāhmaṇas, the Vedas, and sacrifices were established long ago.

**17.24** That is why those who teach brahman always begin the acts of sacrifice, giving, and austerity prescribed by the rule with the word "Om."

**17.25** With the word "tat" ["that"], seekers of liberation perform acts of sacrifice and austerity and various acts of giving without aiming at the fruit.

**17.26** "Sat" is used to mean reality and goodness. In the same way, Pārtha, the word "sat" is used for any praiseworthy act.

**17.27** Steadfastness in sacrifice, austerity, and giving is also called "sat," and so is any action done for their sake.

**17.28** Whatever is offered, given, or done as austerity without faith is called "asat," Pārtha. It counts for nothing, either here or hereafter.

## Chapter 18 · *Mokṣa-saṁnyāsa-yoga* · The Yoga of Liberation through Renunciation

*Arjuna said:*

**18.1** Mighty-armed one, Hṛṣīkeśa, slayer of Keśin, I wish to know the truth about renunciation [*saṁnyāsa*] and about relinquishment [*tyāga*], each on its own.

*The Blessed Lord said:*

**18.2** The wise understand renunciation as giving up actions done from desire. The discerning call giving up the fruit of all actions relinquishment.

**18.3** Some thinkers say that action should be relinquished because it is full of fault. Others say that acts of sacrifice, giving, and austerity should not be relinquished.

**18.4** Hear my conclusion about relinquishment, best of Bhāratas. Relinquishment, tiger among men, is said to be of three kinds.

**18.5** Acts of sacrifice, giving, and austerity should not be relinquished. They should be performed, for sacrifice, giving, and austerity purify the wise.

**18.6** But even these actions should be performed without attachment and without seeking their fruit. This, Pārtha, is my settled and highest view.

**18.7** It is not right to renounce prescribed action. Giving it up out of delusion is said to be of tamas.

**18.8** One who gives up action because it is painful, from fear of bodily hardship, makes a relinquishment of rajas and does not gain the fruit of relinquishment.

**18.9** When prescribed action is done simply because it ought to be done, Arjuna, without attachment and without seeking its fruit, that relinquishment is held to be of sattva.

**18.10** The one who relinquishes in this way, filled with sattva, wise, and with doubts cut away, does not hate unpleasant action or cling to pleasant action.

**18.11** No embodied being can give up actions entirely. But one who gives up the fruit of action is called a relinquisher.

**18.12** For those who have not relinquished, the fruit of action after death is threefold: unwanted, wanted, and mixed. For renunciants there is none at all.

**18.13** Learn from me, mighty-armed one, the five factors that bring about every action, as taught in the Sāṅkhya doctrine:

**18.14** the basis [the body], the agent, the various instruments, the various distinct activities, and fifth, the divine [*daiva*, fate or providence].
> *Note:* *daiva* can mean the gods, unseen causes, fate, or providence. The Gītā does not settle which.

**18.15** Whatever action a person undertakes with body, speech, or mind, right or wrong, these five are its causes.

**18.16** That being so, one who, through an unformed understanding, sees the self alone as the agent does not truly see. That person's mind is perverse.

**18.17** One who is free from the sense of I-making and whose understanding is untainted does not kill and is not bound, even if he kills these people.

**18.18** Knowledge, the object of knowledge, and the knower are the three things that prompt action. The instrument, the act, and the agent are the three parts of action.

**18.19** In the doctrine of the guṇas, knowledge, action, and agent are each said to be of three kinds, according to the guṇas. Hear about these as well.

**18.20** Knowledge of sattva is the knowledge by which one sees one imperishable being in all beings, undivided within the divided.

**18.21** Knowledge of rajas sees in all beings many separate entities of different kinds.

**18.22** Knowledge of tamas clings to one single thing as if it were the whole. It is without reason, does not grasp the truth, and is narrow.

**18.23** Action of sattva is prescribed action, done without attachment, without passion or hatred, by one who does not want its fruit.

**18.24** Action of rajas is done by one who wants to fulfill desires, or out of I-making, with great strain.

**18.25** Action of tamas is undertaken through delusion, with no regard for consequences, loss, harm to others, or one's own ability.

**18.26** The agent of sattva is free of attachment and does not talk about himself, is full of steadfastness and energy, and is unchanged in success and failure.

**18.27** The agent of rajas is passionate and wants the fruit of action. He is greedy, violent, impure, and swings between elation and grief.

**18.28** The agent of tamas is unyoked, crude, stubborn, deceitful, spiteful, lazy, despondent, and always putting things off.

**18.29** Now hear, Dhanañjaya, the threefold division of understanding and of steadfastness [*dhṛti*] according to the guṇas, explained fully and one by one.

**18.30** Understanding of sattva, Pārtha, knows when to engage and when to withdraw, what should and should not be done, what to fear and what not to fear, and what is bondage and what is liberation.

**18.31** Understanding of rajas, Pārtha, misjudges dharma and adharma, and what should and should not be done.

**18.32** Understanding of tamas, Pārtha, is covered by darkness. It takes adharma for dharma and sees all things upside down.

**18.33** Steadfastness of sattva, Pārtha, holds steady the workings of mind, breath, and senses through unwavering yoga.

**18.34** Steadfastness of rajas, Arjuna, holds to dharma, pleasure [*kāma*], and wealth [*artha*] out of attachment and desire for their fruit.

**18.35** Steadfastness of tamas, Pārtha, is that by which the foolish will not let go of sleep, fear, grief, despondency, and arrogance.

**18.36** Now hear from me, bull of the Bhāratas, about the three kinds of happiness. In one of them a person comes to delight through practice and reaches the end of sorrow.

**18.37** Happiness of sattva is like poison at first and like nectar in the end. It is born of the clarity of one's own understanding.

**18.38** Happiness of rajas arises from the meeting of the senses and their objects. It is like nectar at first and like poison in the end.

**18.39** Happiness of tamas deludes the self both at first and in the end. It arises from sleep, laziness, and negligence.

**18.40** There is no being on earth, or among the gods in heaven, that is free of these three guṇas born of nature.

**18.41** The actions of brāhmaṇas, kṣatriyas, vaiśyas, and śūdras, scorcher of foes, are divided according to the guṇas that arise from their own nature [*svabhāva*].

**18.42** Calm, self-restraint, austerity, purity, patience, uprightness, knowledge, discernment, and faith are the action of the brāhmaṇa, born of his own nature.

**18.43** Heroism, vigor, steadfastness, skill, not fleeing in battle, generosity, and leadership are the action of the kṣatriya, born of his own nature.

**18.44** Farming, herding, and trade are the action of the vaiśya, born of his own nature. Action that consists of service is the action of the śūdra, born of his own nature.

**18.45** Each person who delights in his own action reaches perfection. Hear how one devoted to one's own action finds perfection.

**18.46** By worshiping, through one's own action, that from which beings arise and by which all this is pervaded, a person finds perfection.

**18.47** Better one's own dharma, though imperfect, than another's dharma well performed. One who does the action set by one's own nature incurs no fault.

**18.48** One should not give up the action one is born to, son of Kuntī, even if it is flawed. All undertakings are clouded by flaws, as fire is by smoke.

**18.49** With understanding unattached everywhere, self-conquered and free of longing, one reaches through renunciation the supreme perfection of freedom from action [*naiṣkarmya-siddhi*].

**18.50** Learn from me briefly, son of Kuntī, how one who has reached perfection reaches brahman, the highest culmination of knowledge.

**18.51** Yoked with purified understanding, controlling the self with steadfastness, turning away from sound and the other objects of sense, setting aside attraction and aversion,

**18.52** living in solitude, eating lightly, restraining speech, body, and mind, always intent on the yoga of meditation, relying on dispassion,

**18.53** and letting go of I-making, force, arrogance, desire, anger, and possessiveness, without "mine" and at peace, one is fit to become brahman.

**18.54** Having become brahman, serene in self, one neither grieves nor desires. Being the same toward all beings, one reaches supreme devotion to me.

**18.55** Through devotion one knows me in truth: how great I am and who I am. Then, knowing me in truth, one enters into me at once.

**18.56** Though always performing all kinds of actions, one who takes refuge in me reaches by my grace the eternal, imperishable abode.

**18.57** In your mind surrender all actions to me. Hold me as highest, rely on the yoga of understanding, and keep your thought always on me.

**18.58** With your thought on me, you will cross every obstacle by my grace. But if from I-making you will not listen, you will perish.

**18.59** If you rely on I-making and think "I will not fight," your resolve is in vain. Your own nature will compel you.

**18.60** You are bound, son of Kuntī, by your own action, born of your own nature. What you do not wish to do out of delusion, you will do even against your will.

**18.61** The Lord dwells in the heart of all beings, Arjuna. By his māyā he makes all beings revolve, as if mounted on a machine.

**18.62** Take refuge in him alone with your whole being, Bhārata. By his grace you will reach supreme peace and the eternal abode.

**18.63** So I have declared to you knowledge more secret than any secret. Reflect on it fully, then do as you wish.

**18.64** Hear again my supreme word, the most secret of all. You are deeply loved by me, so I will tell you what is good for you.

**18.65** Let your mind be on me; be devoted to me; sacrifice to me; bow down to me. You will come to me. I promise you truly, for you are dear to me.

**18.66** Abandon all dharmas and take refuge in me alone. I will free you from all evils. Do not grieve.
> *Note:* This is the "final verse" (*carama-śloka*) for many traditions. "Abandon all dharmas" has been read as giving up attachment to the results of one's duties, giving up reliance on one's own efforts, or giving up all religious means in favor of grace. The Sanskrit allows each reading.

**18.67** You should never tell this to one without austerity, or without devotion, or to one who does not wish to listen, or to one who speaks ill of me.

**18.68** One who teaches this supreme secret to my devotees, having shown the highest devotion to me, will come to me without doubt.

**18.69** No one among humans does anything dearer to me than that person, and no one on earth will be dearer to me.

**18.70** One who studies this dialogue of ours on dharma will have worshiped me with the sacrifice of knowledge. This is my view.

**18.71** And a person who listens to it with faith and without finding fault will also be freed and will reach the auspicious worlds of those whose deeds are good.

**18.72** Have you listened to this, Pārtha, with one-pointed thought? Has your delusion born of ignorance been destroyed, Dhanañjaya?

*Arjuna said:*

**18.73** My delusion is destroyed, and through your grace, Acyuta, I have regained my memory [*smṛti*]. I stand firm, my doubts gone. I will do as you say.

*Sañjaya said:*

**18.74** So I have heard this wondrous dialogue between Vāsudeva and the great-souled Pārtha, which makes my hair stand on end.

**18.75** By the grace of Vyāsa I heard this supreme secret, this yoga, from Kṛṣṇa himself, the lord of yoga, as he declared it in person.

**18.76** O king, as I remember again and again this wondrous and holy dialogue between Keśava and Arjuna, I rejoice again and again.

**18.77** And as I remember again and again that most wondrous form of Hari, great is my amazement, O king, and I rejoice again and again.

**18.78** Wherever there is Kṛṣṇa, the lord of yoga, and wherever there is Pārtha, the archer, there, I believe, are fortune, victory, prosperity, and sound policy [*nīti*].

---

*Oṁ tat sat.*
